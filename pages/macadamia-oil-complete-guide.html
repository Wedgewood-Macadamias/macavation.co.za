Macadamia Oil Benefits

Of all the products derived from the macadamia nut, oil is perhaps the most versatile and the most misunderstood. It outperforms olive oil at high-heat cooking. It rivals argan oil in skin applications. It is prized by chefs for its flavour, by formulators for its stability, and by dermatologists for its skin-compatible fatty acid profile.

Yet macadamia oil remains relatively unknown compared to the oils it outperforms in several key measures.

This guide sets the record straight — covering everything from the chemistry of macadamia oil to its culinary, nutritional, and cosmetic applications, and explaining why Macavation produces three distinct grades of macadamia oil to serve different needs.

What Is Macadamia Oil?

Macadamia oil is cold-pressed or refined from macadamia kernels — the same creamy nut eaten as a snack or used in confectionery. Depending on the extraction method and degree of refinement, the resulting oil ranges from a pale golden, flavour-rich extra-virgin product to a light, neutral, highly stable refined oil suitable for industrial food applications and cosmetics.

The oil accounts for roughly 75–78% of the macadamia kernel's weight — making macadamias the most oil-dense nut in common cultivation, and making macadamia oil extraction an efficient proposition for processors.

The Fatty Acid Profile: Why Macadamia Oil Is Different

The nutritional value and functional properties of any oil are largely determined by its fatty acid composition. Here is where macadamia oil genuinely stands apart:

Oleic Acid (Omega-9): ~58–65%

Oleic acid is the dominant fatty acid in macadamia oil — the same monounsaturated fat that makes olive oil cardioprotective. Oleic acid is highly stable, resistant to oxidation, and associated with reduced LDL cholesterol and improved cardiovascular risk markers. In macadamia oil, it typically represents a slightly higher percentage than in olive oil.

Palmitoleic Acid (Omega-7): ~17–22%

This is the compound that makes macadamia oil truly distinctive. Palmitoleic acid is a monounsaturated omega-7 fatty acid that is extremely rare in plant-based oils. Macadamia oil is the richest known plant source of palmitoleic acid, typically containing 17–22% — a quantity unmatched by any other common culinary oil.

Palmitoleic acid has several documented and emerging benefits:

  • It is a natural component of human sebum, making macadamia oil highly compatible with skin physiology
  • It has demonstrated anti-inflammatory properties in research contexts
  • It may support insulin sensitivity and metabolic function
  • In cosmetic applications, it is associated with skin softening, anti-ageing effects, and barrier restoration

Saturated Fat: ~12–15%

This is the compound that makes macadamia oil truly distinctive. Palmitoleic acid is a monounsaturated omega-7 fatty acid that is extremely rare in plant-based oils. Macadamia oil is the richest known plant source of palmitoleic acid, typically containing 17–22% — a quantity unmatched by any other common culinary oil.

Omega-6 (Linoleic Acid): ~1–3%

This is perhaps the most underappreciated aspect of macadamia oil's profile. Most common cooking oils — sunflower, corn, soybean, safflower — are 50–70% omega-6, contributing to the inflammatory omega-6 excess that characterises most Western diets. Macadamia oil contains only 1–3% omega-6, making it one of the lowest-omega-6 culinary oils available. For anyone seeking to reduce inflammatory fatty acid imbalance in their diet, this is significant.

Omega-3: ~0–1%

Macadamia oil is not a significant source of omega-3 (unlike flaxseed, walnut, or hemp oil), but its very low omega-6 content means that the omega-6 to omega-3 ratio remains favourable — not because of omega-3 abundance, but because of omega-6 restraint.

Macadamia Oil vs Olive Oil: An Honest Comparison

This is one of the most searched questions in premium oil categories, and the answer is nuanced — both are excellent oils, but they have different strengths.

  • Property | Macadamia Oil | Extra Virgin Olive Oil
  • Smoke point | 210–240°C (refined) | 160–190°C (EVOO)
  • Oleic acid | 58–65% | 55–83%
  • Palmitoleic acid | 17–22% | 0.3–3%
  • Omega-6 content | 1–3% | 4–10%
  • Oxidative stability | Very high | High (EVOO), variable
  • Flavour | Mild, buttery, neutral (refined) | Grassy, peppery, fruity
  • Cosmetic use | Excellent (skin-compatible) | Moderate (heavier texture)
  • Price | Premium | Mid to premium

Where Macadamia Oil Wins

High-heat cooking. The smoke point of refined macadamia oil (210–240°C) significantly exceeds that of extra virgin olive oil (160–190°C). At temperatures above the smoke point, oils begin to break down, producing harmful compounds and losing their nutritional benefits. For stir-frying, searing, roasting, or deep-frying, macadamia oil is the superior choice. Extra virgin olive oil, despite its excellent nutritional profile, is genuinely not ideal for high-heat applications.

Oxidative stability. Oils high in polyunsaturated fats (especially omega-6) oxidise quickly when exposed to heat, light, or air — producing rancid, inflammatory compounds. Macadamia oil's very high monounsaturated fat content and extremely low polyunsaturated fat content make it exceptionally resistant to oxidation. It has a longer shelf life than most culinary oils and maintains its quality better under cooking conditions.

Skin compatibility. The palmitoleic acid content of macadamia oil makes it uniquely compatible with human skin physiology in a way that olive oil is not. Olive oil's oleic acid content can disrupt skin barrier function in some people; macadamia oil is lighter, more readily absorbed, and closely matched to the skin's natural sebum.

Neutral flavour. Refined macadamia oil has a very mild, slightly buttery flavour that does not compete with other ingredients. For applications where a neutral oil is needed — baking, dressings where other flavours should dominate, cosmetic formulations — macadamia oil is the better choice.

Where Olive Oil Wins

Polyphenol content. High-quality extra virgin olive oil contains a wider range of polyphenols (particularly oleocanthal and oleuropein) that have documented anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties. Extra-virgin macadamia oil contains antioxidants, but the polyphenol profile of EVOO is more extensively researched and in some contexts superior.

Tradition and accessibility. Olive oil has centuries of culinary tradition behind it, and its health benefits are backed by the extensive Mediterranean diet literature. It is also widely available and, at volume, more affordable than premium macadamia oil.

Raw and finishing applications. The distinctive grassy, peppery flavour of good extra virgin olive oil is an asset when the oil is meant to be tasted — on bread, in vinaigrettes, drizzled over finished dishes. Where flavour is part of the point, EVOO often wins on character.

The verdict: These are complementary oils, not competitors. Use macadamia oil for high-heat cooking, baking, and applications where stability and skin-compatibility matter. Use extra virgin olive oil for flavour-forward raw applications, dressings, and finishing. Both deserve a place in a well-equipped kitchen.

Macavation's Three Oil Grades

Macavation produces three distinct grades of macadamia oil to serve different applications:

Extra-Virgin Macadamia Oil

Cold-pressed from premium macadamia kernels without heat or chemical processing, extra-virgin macadamia oil retains the full flavour, colour, and nutritional profile of the fresh kernel. It has a light golden colour and a delicate, slightly buttery flavour.

Best for: Premium food applications, raw dressings, health-conscious consumers, finishing oil, foodservice, and specialist retail. Also suitable for cosmetic formulations where a naturally processed, full-spectrum oil is preferred.

Refined Macadamia Oil

Refined using gentle processing that removes impurities, free fatty acids, and flavour compounds while preserving the fatty acid profile. The result is a light, neutral, highly stable oil suitable for a very wide range of applications.

Best for: High-heat cooking, industrial food manufacturing, emulsified products like mayonnaise, baked goods where a neutral flavour is required, and cosmetic formulations requiring a consistent, light-textured carrier oil.

Cosmetic-Grade Macadamia Oil

Processed specifically for cosmetic and personal care applications, meeting purity standards required for inclusion in skincare, haircare, and cosmetic formulations. The high palmitoleic acid content and skin-compatible fatty acid profile make this one of the most valued carrier oils in the cosmetic industry.

Best for: Moisturisers, serums, face oils, body oils, hair treatments, lip products, sun care, and any cosmetic formulation requiring a lightweight, non-comedogenic, skin-nourishing carrier.

Culinary Applications: Getting the Most From Macadamia Oil

High-Heat Cooking

The high smoke point and oxidative stability of refined macadamia oil make it ideal for:

  • Stir-frying and wok cooking
  • Searing and browning meat, fish, or vegetables
  • Roasting at high oven temperatures (200°C+)
  • Deep frying (it remains stable at temperatures required for proper deep frying)
  • Grilling and griddle cooking

Baking

Macadamia oil is an excellent substitute for butter or other oils in baking. Its neutral flavour does not compete with other ingredients, it remains stable at baking temperatures, and it adds moisture without heaviness. Particularly well-suited to:

  • Muffins, cakes, and quick breads
  • Biscuits and cookies
  • Pastry applications

Dressings and Sauces

Extra-virgin macadamia oil's mild buttery flavour makes it an excellent base for:

  • Vinaigrettes and salad dressings
  • Mayonnaise (Macavation has developed bespoke macadamia mayonnaise recipes — both vegan and egg-based — that leverage the oil's emulsification properties and heart-healthy profile)
  • Dipping oils and compound preparations
  • Finishing oil for pasta, seafood, and vegetable dishes

Food Formulation and Manufacturing

For food manufacturers and product developers, refined macadamia oil offers:

  • A premium, differentiated oil with a compelling health story
  • Exceptional oxidative stability for extended shelf life in finished products
  • A low omega-6 profile that aligns with anti-inflammatory and clean-label formulation trends
  • Compatibility with a wide range of processing conditions

Macavation works directly with food manufacturers to supply macadamia oil at the volumes and specifications required for commercial production.

Cosmetic Applications: The Skin Science

The cosmetic use of macadamia oil is among the most scientifically grounded applications in natural skin care — and it is worth understanding why.

Sebum Compatibility

Human sebum — the skin's natural oil — contains palmitoleic acid as one of its primary fatty acid components. As we age, sebum production and palmitoleic acid content decline, which is one of the mechanisms behind age-related skin dryness and reduced barrier function. Macadamia oil, with its 17–22% palmitoleic acid content, closely mimics the skin's natural oil — which is why it absorbs so readily and leaves the skin feeling nourished rather than greasy.

This makes macadamia oil particularly beneficial for:

  • Mature or ageing skin
  • Dry or dehydrated skin
  • Post-sun exposure skin care
  • Eczema-prone or sensitive skin that benefits from barrier support

Non-Comedogenic Properties

Despite being an oil, macadamia oil rates as non-comedogenic or very low-comedogenic — meaning it does not clog pores. This makes it suitable for use on facial skin, including for people concerned about breakouts from heavier oils.

Hair and Scalp Applications

The fatty acid profile of macadamia oil makes it an excellent conditioning agent for hair. It:

  • Smooths the cuticle, reducing frizz and improving shine
  • Penetrates the hair shaft more effectively than heavier oils (like coconut oil)
  • Conditions the scalp without leaving a heavy residue
  • Protects hair from heat styling damage when applied as a pre-treatment
